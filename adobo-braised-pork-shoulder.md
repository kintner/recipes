# Adobo Braised Pork Shoulder

An all-raw, covered-oven braise designed to approximate the soft, savory pork in CookUnity's Adobo Braised Pork Shoulder by Chef Stacy Bareng. This is a reconstruction, not the chef's published recipe.

**Target:** Rich pork that collapses into shreds under a fork, with soy, garlic, gentle sweetness, and enough acidity to avoid tasting flat without a distinct vinegar tang.

- **Yield:** About 4 servings  
- **Pork:** 2 pounds boneless pork shoulder country ribs, already cut into 5 strips  
- **Prep:** About 15 minutes  
- **Cooking:** Roughly 3-4 hours, plus sauce finishing; tenderness determines timing  
- **Oven:** 300 F / 150 C  
- **Equipment:** A snug oven-safe pot with an oven-safe lid, or a deep baking dish sealed tightly with foil

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Starting amount |
| :---- | ----: |
| Boneless pork shoulder country ribs | 2 lb / about 900 g, in 5 strips |
| Yellow onion, thinly sliced | 1 medium |
| Garlic, smashed and roughly chopped | 8 cloves |
| Light soy sauce, regular strength | 1/4 cup / 60 ml |
| Food-grade distilled white vinegar, 5% acidity | 2 tablespoons / 30 ml |
| Light brown sugar, packed | 1 1/2 tablespoons / about 20 g |
| Bay leaves | 2 |
| Coarsely ground black pepper | 1/2 teaspoon |
| Water | 1 cup / 240 ml initially, plus enough to reach the level below |

**Soy sauce:** "Light" describes the style, not necessarily reduced sodium. Regular all-purpose soy sauce also works. Do not substitute dark soy sauce in the same quantity. Add no salt initially; adjust after cooking.  
**Lemon juice:** Leave it out of the first batch. The supplied ingredient list covers the entire meal, so it does not establish whether lemon belongs in the pork or another component.

## Method

1. **Preheat and prepare.** Heat the oven to 300 F / 150 C. Keep the pork in its five strips. Trim only thick, hard exterior fat; leave the marbling and connective tissue.  
     
2. **Combine everything raw.** In the cooking vessel, mix the water, soy sauce, vinegar, sugar, and pepper. Add the onion, garlic, bay leaves, and pork. Arrange the strips in a snug layer where possible. Add enough extra water to reach **halfway to two-thirds up the meat**, rather than fully submerging it. A snug vessel helps avoid an unnecessarily diluted sauce.  
     
3. **Cover tightly and braise.** Cover with the lid or tightly crimped foil and put directly into the preheated oven. Turn the strips after about 90 minutes to 2 hours if convenient. If the liquid is getting low, add a little hot water and reseal.  
     
4. **Cook to the texture, not the clock.** Start checking at about 2 1/2 hours; expect roughly 3-4 hours from a cold-liquid start, depending on strip thickness and the cooking vessel. Press a fork into the thickest meaty portion: it should **collapse into strands with almost no resistance**. If it feels springy or merely slices neatly, continue in 20-30-minute increments. If it becomes tender sooner, stop sooner. Internal temperature often reaches roughly 195-205 F / 90-96 C, but that range is a guide, not a substitute for the tenderness check.  
     
5. **Finish the sauce separately.** Carefully lift out the tender pork and discard the bay leaves. Using a fine meshed strainer, strain the sauce into a sauce pan and discard the aromatics. Refrigerate pork and sauce overnight. The next morning, divide pork into separate servings for freezing. For the sauce, remove and discard solidified fat. It’s ok if a little remains. Simmer uncovered until the sauce is no longer weak and watery, tasting as it concentrates. Add vinegar ½ tsp at a time until the sauce is bright but not acidic; add salt as needed.   
     
6. **Serving.** Reheat the pork in the toaster oven at 350F for roughly 12 minutes. The pork should be crispy and fork tender. Add sauce and serve.
